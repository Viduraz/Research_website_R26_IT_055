Platform & Research Domain

How four AI research components work together to support elder-care monitoring.

Research domain

Safer care through movement awareness

Continuous observation can be difficult in home and assisted-care environments. This project studies how computer vision can help caregivers notice changes in movement, location and daily activities while considering privacy and practical deployment constraints.

The work combines four independently developed services in a video-based monitoring platform. Their outputs support caregiver awareness and review; they do not replace professional care.

Research focus

  • Identification through face and body movement cues
  • Persistent person tracking and configurable safety zones
  • Temporal pose analysis for unusual movement
  • Care routine matching and missed-activity review

Component research

What each service contributes

Himaya Jayasinghe · IT22240392

Skeleton-Based Identification & Gait Analysis

MediaPipe 3D pose landmarks provide world-coordinate body measurements. A 29-dimensional anthropometric representation describes body proportions; a 30-frame window supplies gait kinematics for temporal matching. The documented design combines a biometric prototype matcher, SVM static classifier and bidirectional LSTM gait model, with an open-set rejection path for uncertain or unknown identities.

Privacy aim: identify through body geometry and gait when facial visibility is poor. Reported result: 94.2% field identification and 91.8% face-occluded identification in the supplied component specification; see evaluation caveats on the results page.

D.M.D.L. Dedunu · IT22171788

Tracking, Geofencing & Caregiver Continuity

YOLOv8 Nano detections are tracked with ByteTrack or BoT-SORT, with IoU and centroid matching as a fallback. Administrators can configure polygonal safe, restricted and alert zones. Posture and estimated distance inform zone-breach decisions; trajectory and box-size changes support room-exit alerts.

Caregiver visibility checks raise staged absence states after 10, 30 and 120 seconds. The tracking service also requests verified identity from the face service to associate names and roles with track IDs.

Documented settings: detection confidence above 0.30; active tracks are retained for 1.5 seconds and stale tracks purged after 3 seconds. These values describe the supplied implementation notes.

Project Leader · R.M.V.R. Rathnayaka · IT22075208

Pose-Based Anomaly Detection

MediaPipe landmarks are converted into temporal skeletal features. A PyTorch LSTM classifies supported events such as falls, aggression and prolonged inactivity; an autoencoder reconstruction signal and threshold logic support unusual-pattern checks.

Chamathka B.M.G.N. · IT22143686

Schedule & Activity Monitoring

A browser-based monitoring pipeline combines MediaPipe BlazePose landmarks, face landmarks and COCO-SSD object context with temporal geometry and motion rules. Activity observations include walking, sitting/resting, sleeping, eating and drinking, then match against routines.

Routine outcomes include early, completed, late, missed and caregiver-missing states. Event records feed activity history, notifications, and daily or weekly reports. The current source has optional Random Forest and LSTM paths, while their feature flags are off; the active path is the browser-based pose and rule pipeline.
